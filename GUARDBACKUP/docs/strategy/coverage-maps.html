Loc8 coverage — the case for anchors

Same festival, same crowd, three infrastructure choices. Green = a person there can reliably reach the mesh.

📍 modelled on All Points East · Victoria Park, London · 50,000 cap · ~600 × 450 m arena
Option 1 · phones only

Just the app

Works only where the crowd is dense enough for phones to chain together. Thins out and dies at the edges.

Coverage~60%
Anchors to deploy0
Hardware cost£0
Fails whereedges · quiet corners · day 1
Option 2 · + BLE anchors

Relay grid

Always-on Bluetooth relays on a grid fill the gaps. Solid coverage, but every unit needs power.

Coverage~90%
Anchors to deploy~32 (1 / 90 m)
Hardware cost~£1k (£30 ea)
Catchpower + mounting × 32
Option 3 · + LoRa backbone

Trunk + last-hop

A dozen long-range LoRa nodes span the whole park and bridge distant clusters. Fewest units, full coverage, resilient even at low crowd.

Coverage~98%
Anchors to deploy~13 trunk
Hardware cost~£1k (£60–80 ea)
Bonusworks before anyone installs
mesh coverage stage BLE anchor LoRa trunk node boating lake dead zone
Model: BLE reliable range ≈ 30 m in a packed crowd (bodies absorb 2.4 GHz), up to ~80 m open. Anchor spacing / counts are planning estimates for this footprint, not a survey.
The honest caveat holds in all three: a backgrounded iPhone a non-Apple anchor has never linked to stays partly invisible — anchors help most once a link exists and for Android.
