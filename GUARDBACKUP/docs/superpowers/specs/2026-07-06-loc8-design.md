# Loc8 — Design Spec

**Date:** 2026-07-06 (revised same day — v2 of this doc, incorporating external reviews + gap audit)
**Status:** Approved direction, final review pending
**One-liner:** Find your crew at a festival when the data's dead — a live Bluetooth-mesh radar that works with zero signal.

---

## 1. Problem & vision

At crowded events (festivals, stadiums, protests) cell networks get **congested** and data effectively dies — not because there's no tower, but because thousands of phones overwhelm it. Finding your friends becomes impossible right when you most need to.

**Key insight:** GPS is receive-only from satellites and works with **zero cell signal** — every phone already knows its *own* location offline. The only hard problem is *sharing* that ~20-byte location between friends without the internet. At a dense event there are thousands of phones packed together, which makes a **phone-to-phone Bluetooth mesh** the natural transport: the very density that kills the cell network is what makes the mesh strong.

**Loc8** is a cross-platform (iOS + Android) mobile app that shows your crew on a live radar and relays everyone's location over an offline BLE mesh.

**What "game-changing" means here, concretely:**
- It works *precisely when everything else fails* — the moment of failure is the moment of magic.
- The mesh gets *stronger* with density (network effect baked into physics).
- The final 20 meters — where GPS apps traditionally fall apart — is designed as its own experience (§3, Proximity mode), not an afterthought.
- Finding someone ends in a *celebration*, not a shrug. The product's emotional peak is engineered, not accidental.

---

## 2. Goals & non-goals

### Goals
- See your whole crew's live direction + distance with no internet.
- Hone in on one friend with a big compass arrow; graceful hand-off to "look around" proximity mode at close range.
- One-tap **Ping** ("come find me" / "where are you?") that works even for invisible-mode friends.
- Drop a shared "meet here" rally pin the whole crew sees.
- Work when phones are in pockets (background) *where the OS allows*, with honest degradation and privacy controls.
- Cross-platform: an iPhone and an Android in the same crew must find each other.
- Never be the reason someone's phone dies (battery guardrails are a feature, not a caveat).

### Non-goals (v1)
- Real Bluetooth mesh (v1 uses a **simulated** transport — §8).
- Offline map tiles (the Radar UI is designed to avoid needing them).
- Satellite / off-grid wilderness use (documented as a v3 fallback — §11).
- Chat / messaging, social features, accounts beyond a name + avatar.
- Multiple simultaneous crews, breadcrumb trails, dead-reckoning/sensor-fusion (v3 polish).

## 3. Core experience

### Onboarding & permissions (first run)
The app is dead without Location + Bluetooth permissions, and iOS gives one good shot at asking. First-run flow:
1. **Hook screen:** "Find your crew when the signal's gone." Create profile: name + avatar color. No account, no email.
2. **Permission priming, one at a time, each with a why:**
   - *Location:* "Loc8 uses your GPS to show your crew where you are. Your location is end-to-end encrypted — it never leaves your crew." → then the OS prompt.
   - *Bluetooth (v2, at first mesh use):* "This is how Loc8 works with no internet — phones relay for each other." → then the OS prompt.
   - *Notifications (deferred until first ping/rally):* asked in context, when the value is obvious.
3. Denied permission → a clear, non-nagging recovery screen explaining what's broken and a deep-link to Settings.

### Radar (home screen)
- **You** are the pulsing dot in the center. **North-up orientation** (rock-steady; no magnetometer jitter — heading is only used in Compass mode).
- **Crew members** orbit you at their real bearing + distance, updating live.
- **Freshness is always visible.** Every blip carries its age ("3s" / "4m"). Data older than ~90s desaturates; a friend who's stopped broadcasting fades to a ghosted blip pinned at their **last known position** with "last seen 4m ago." Stale data is never silently presented as live.
- A **"MESH · N nearby"** badge shows connection state. Friends reached via relay show "via ___'s phone" — the mesh made visible.
- **Distance scaling is piecewise:** linear out to ~150m (where precision matters), logarithmic beyond (so far friends don't pile on the outer ring). Rings labeled honestly.
- Bottom sheet lists the crew (name, distance, direction, freshness) with **Find →** and **Ping** actions.

### Compass (tap a friend)
- Full-screen arrow pointing at that one person; distance counts down as you walk.
- Arrow rotation = smoothed device heading vs. bearing (low-pass filtered — §6).
- **Warmth feedback:** "🧭 keep walking" → "🔥 getting warmer" → close-range hand-off:

### Proximity mode (the final 20 meters)
GPS accuracy in dense crowds degrades to 10–30m — at close range an arrow is a lie. So below ~25m (tuned against reported GPS accuracy), Compass **gracefully switches modes**: the arrow dissolves into an expanding pulse — **"You're basically there — look around 👀"** — with haptic pulses that quicken as fresh packets arrive. Both phones buzz.
- **Found-each-other moment:** when two users who were finding each other close within ~15m, both get a full-screen 🎉 **"You found each other!"** celebration (confetti, haptic burst). This is the product's emotional payoff and its most shareable moment — engineered, not left to chance.

### Ping (one-tap summon)
- **"Where are you?"** ping → friend's phone buzzes with your name; one tap replies with their live location (this is also *the* answer mechanism for Invisible mode — §5).
- **"Come find me"** ping → sends your position + a notification that deep-links straight into Compass mode pointed at you.
- Pings are just another tiny packet type over the same transport. Rate-limited (no ping-spam) and silenceable per-friend.

### Rally pin
- Anyone drops a **"meet here"** flag; everyone's radar shows it with an arrow + distance.
- **Rule: one active pin per crew, latest wins, labeled with who dropped it** ("🚩 Maya · Meet here"). Dropping a new pin replaces the old one and notifies the crew. Prevents pin chaos.

## 4. Crew setup & session lifecycle

### Two joining paths
- **Beforehand (online, primary):** Create a crew while you still have signal → friends join via link/code → identities + E2E keys exchanged and cached locally → works fully offline later.
- **In-person QR (offline):** Scan each other's QR to swap keys over Bluetooth with zero signal — for people you meet on the ground.
  - **v1 note:** QR flow is **mocked** in the simulated prototype; real pairing lands with the real transport in v2.

### Session lifecycle (battery + privacy by design)
"Broadcasting" is never an ambient forever-state; it's bounded by an explicit **session**:
- Starting a session asks for a duration — default **"until 6h from now"**, with presets (4h / 12h / weekend).
- Sessions **auto-expire**: broadcasting stops, a notification confirms it. No "I forgot Loc8 was tracking me the morning after" — that's both a battery guarantee and a privacy promise.
- One tap extends a session; ending early is equally easy. Crew members see who's in/out of session.

## 5. Sharing, privacy & packet trust

### Visibility (three-position switch + go dark)
- **Live (default):** broadcast during an active session, background where the OS allows (§7 risk). Radar stays true in-pocket, best effort.
- **Only while open:** battery-saver; visible only with Loc8 on screen.
- **Invisible / on-demand:** you disappear; you show up only when you tap "share" or **answer a ping** (§3). One-tap **"go dark"** always available.

### Trust model
- **All location data is end-to-end encrypted to your crew only.** The mesh transport is treated as **untrusted** — we encrypt/authenticate at the app layer regardless of engine (non-negotiable given Bridgefy's security history — §9).
- **Replay protection:** relayed packets can be captured and re-broadcast to make you appear where you were an hour ago. Receivers **reject any packet older than N minutes or older than the last-seen timestamp for that sender**. Cheap, closes the hole.
- **Ordering / clock skew:** relayed packets arrive late and out of order (Sam's position hopping three phones can arrive *after* a newer direct one). Rule: **last-write-wins by sender timestamp**, and the UI always displays freshness so lateness is visible, never hidden.
- **No analytics, no server-side location storage.** Positions live on crew members' devices, period. (The optional v3 cloud sync (§11) is opt-in and stores only what the user chooses.)

### Battery guardrails
- **Adaptive update rate:** ~1 packet/5s when moving, backing off to ~1/30s when stationary.
- **Low-battery beacon mode:** below 15% battery, automatically drop to ~1 packet/60s and notify the user ("Loc8 is in power-saver — you're still findable"). *"Loc8 killed my phone" must never be the review.*
- Session auto-expiry (§4) is the backstop.

## 6. Location tech & math

- **GPS** via `expo-location` — works offline; each packet carries reported accuracy.
- **Packet format (~20 bytes):** senderId, lat, lon, heading, battery, timestamp, accuracy, packetType (position / ping / rally / ack). Tiny deliberately, so it fits a **connectionless BLE advertisement** — no connection handshake, meshes past the ~8-connection hardware ceiling; hundreds of phones can sniff the advert. ⚠️ *Caveat:* connectionless advertising is exactly what iOS hides in the background "overflow area" (§7) — the scaling win is a **foreground** property; backgrounded iPhone↔Android relies on Screen-On Relay.
- **Human-readable share layer — Plus Codes** (Open Location Code): free, fully offline, no license (chosen over what3words's paid API). Tap a friend/pin → shareable `849VCWC8+R9` to shout or text. Internally everything is raw lat/long.

### Rendering math (custom SVG — no map library)
- **Distance:** Haversine. **Bearing:** forward azimuth (0–360°, 0° = true north).
- **Plot:** piecewise distance scale (§3) → pixel radius → polar→cartesian (`x = r·sin θ`, `y = −r·cos θ`).
- **North-up radar** (no magnetometer). **Compass mode only** uses heading, passed through a **low-pass / exponential-moving-average filter** with 359°→0° wrap-around handling — festival environments (stages, rigging, battery packs) make raw headings jitter violently. *(Credit: external reviews, 2026-07-06.)*
- All of it lives in a pure-TS **`geoMath` module** — zero dependencies, fully unit-tested (§10). A verified reference implementation exists and drops in at implementation time.

## 7. The hard risk — iOS background advertising ("overflow area")

Verified from Apple's Core Bluetooth docs: when an iOS app advertises **in the background**, its custom service UUID moves into a proprietary "overflow" area that **Android cannot decode**. A backgrounded iPhone in a pocket is invisible to Android scanners, and visible to other iPhones only if they explicitly scan for the exact UUID. OS-level; affects every engine including Bridgefy. This is the single biggest threat to "Live (background)" for the iPhone↔Android case.

**Primary mitigation — "Screen-On Relay" (design around it, don't fight it):** never promise always-on background mesh for cross-platform crews. Instead, **opening Loc8 turns your phone into a temporary foreground router** — it actively scans, wakes nearby devices, and pulls the freshest cached telemetry from the local mesh. You open the app *because* you want to find someone, so the foreground burst arrives exactly when freshness matters. Background still works well iOS↔iOS and Android↔Android; cross-platform-in-pocket degrades gracefully to last-known-position + freshness labels (§3) until someone opens the app. *(Credit: external review, 2026-07-06.)*

**Secondary:** constrain background to same-platform pairs; validate everything in the v2 spike (§9) before promising any background behavior in marketing or UI copy.

## 8. Architecture — phased, transport swappable

The networking layer is a swappable module behind one interface. Nothing in the UI knows how packets move.

```
interface LocationTransport {
  start(session): void
  stop(): void
  broadcast(packet): void            // position | ping | rally
  onPacket(cb): void                 // deduped, replay-checked, ordered
  onRelayInfo(cb): void              // "reached via X" hop metadata
  onMeshStatus(cb): void             // nearby count, connectivity state
}
```

- **v1 — `SimulatedTransport`:** deterministic-seedable mock friends doing a **random-walk on real lat/long** (~1.3 m/s festival amble), emitting real packets through the real pipeline — exercising `geoMath`, freshness/staleness, replay/ordering logic, ping round-trips, and Plus Code conversion. Includes scripted scenarios: "friend goes dark," "friend approaches → proximity → found 🎉," "relayed friend with laggy packets." Runs in Expo Go/dev client. The whole product experience ships and gets validated **before any BLE exists**.
- **v2 — `MeshTransport`:** real BLE mesh engine behind the same interface; UI unchanged. Engine decision in §9.
- **v3 —** off-grid fallbacks (§11).

Component boundaries (each independently testable):
- `geoMath` (pure TS) · `PacketCodec` (encode/decode/validate ~20-byte packets) · `CrewStore` (identities, keys, session state) · `TrustLayer` (encrypt/decrypt, replay/ordering rules) · `Transport` (simulated ↔ mesh) · UI (Radar / Compass / Proximity / Crew / modals).

## 9. v2 mesh engine decision (researched 2026-07-06, six verified reports)

| Option | iOS↔Android offline | Multi-hop | RN/Expo | License | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **bitchat** | ✅ proven | ✅ 7 hops | ❌ native Swift+Kotlin, bridge needed | Unlicense (iOS) / GPL-3 (Android) | **Best OSS fit** — 26k★, very active |
| **Bridgefy** | ✅ | ✅ + broadcast, crowd profile | ⚠️ official RN SDK, no Expo plugin | Paid, opaque MAU pricing | **Fastest turnkey** |
| **Ditto** | ✅ (BLE) | ✅ | ✅ official Expo plugin | Paid, "contact sales" | Easiest Expo; sync-DB overkill |
| Berty/Wesh | ⚠️ flaky | ✅ | ⚠️ gomobile | Apache/MIT | Stalled |
| Google Nearby | ❌ (iOS Wi-Fi-only) | ❌ | ⚠️ | Closed | Ruled out |
| Apple Multipeer | ❌ Apple-only | ❌ | ❌ dead bridges | Free | Ruled out |
| Briar/qaul/Reticulum/Serval/Meshtastic | mixed | some | ❌ wrong stack | GPL/AGPL | Android-only / LoRa hardware / dead |

**Decision:** v1 simulated first → **spike wrapping bitchat's BLE layer** to prove iPhone↔Android background relay for tiny packets → if painful on iOS (likely), **adopt Bridgefy** for v2, keep bitchat as the free long-term target, Ditto as fallback. Roll-our-own (ble-plx is central-only; no maintained cross-platform peripheral lib) is explicitly avoided.

## 10. Quality: testing, error handling, performance

### Testing
- **`geoMath`:** unit tests against known coordinate pairs → known distances/bearings; wrap-around cases (359°→0°, equator/meridian crossings); smoothing filter convergence.
- **`PacketCodec`:** round-trip encode/decode, boundary values, malformed-input rejection.
- **`TrustLayer`:** replay rejection, out-of-order handling, last-write-wins, expired-session rejection.
- **`SimulatedTransport`:** deterministic seeds → reproducible walks, so UI states are reproducible in tests and demos.
- **UI states:** every screen has a defined state for: no permissions, no crew, empty radar, all-stale crew, going dark, low battery, session expired.
- Manual test script for the clickable milestones (tap-through checklist matching §12).

### Error handling (never blank, never lie)
- Permission denied → recovery screen with Settings deep-link (§3).
- GPS unavailable/low accuracy → radar shows accuracy halo; Compass refuses to fake precision (falls to Proximity mode early).
- Mesh empty ("MESH · 0 nearby") → honest state: "No crew in range — last known positions shown."
- Session expired mid-use → clear banner + one-tap restart.

### Performance targets
- Radar animation at 60fps on mid-range devices (Reanimated on UI thread; no per-frame JS bridge crossings).
- Blip updates interpolate smoothly between packets (no teleporting dots).
- Cold start < 2s to radar.

## 11. v3 / future (documented, not built)

- **Offline-first cloud sync (secondary):** cache last-known positions + breadcrumbs locally; opt-in sync (Supabase/PowerSync-style) when signal returns. Complements the mesh; never replaces it.
- **Satellite / NTN off-grid mode (Android-first):** Android 15+ NTN APIs allow tiny text payloads over satellite on capable hardware (Pixel 9+) — an opt-in "off-grid beacon" texting your Plus Code from the wilderness. Android-only, hardware/carrier-gated, tiny payloads; iOS users fall back to OS-level Find My/Messages-over-satellite manually. Solves the *opposite* problem from the festival core (emptiness vs. congestion); stretch feature.
- **Hardware mesh (goTenna/LoRa):** ~0.5–1 mile per hop via add-on radio; companion-device direction if phone-only mesh proves insufficient.
- **Dead-reckoning/sensor fusion, breadcrumb trails, multi-crew, offline map layer:** all real, all later.

## 12. v1 milestone — definition of done

A polished, clickable prototype demoing the full experience with simulated friends:
- Onboarding: profile + permission-priming flow (real permission requests wired).
- Radar home: 3–4 seeded simulated crew moving live; freshness labels; stale/ghost states; relay "via ___" labels; piecewise distance scaling.
- Compass: live smoothed arrow + converging distance; warmth feedback.
- Proximity mode: <25m hand-off, haptics, and the found-each-other 🎉 celebration.
- Ping: both directions, with notification deep-link into Compass (simulated round-trip).
- Rally pin: drop/replace/one-per-crew rule, labeled by dropper.
- Crew screen: session lifecycle (start with duration, extend, expire), mocked create-link + mocked QR-add.
- Privacy: three-position switch + go dark; invisible mode answerable via ping.
- Battery: low-battery beacon mode simulated and visible in UI.
- Plus Code share sheet for any friend or pin.
- Everything behind `LocationTransport`; `SimulatedTransport` deterministic-seedable with scripted demo scenarios.
- Test suite green: `geoMath`, `PacketCodec`, `TrustLayer`, transport determinism.
