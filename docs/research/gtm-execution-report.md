### 4. Delete the SAG lost-child pitch today. It is refuted, and it was about to be the centrepiece of your deck.

The two quotes are real and verbatim — I confirmed both PDFs. **The story built on them is not.**

- **Cambridge City Council SAG** (PDF created 18 Aug 2025, current): *"Pass on all relevant information to security & stewarding DO NOT give out the child's name over an open communications system"* `[HARD DATA]`. **What you may still use:** the Cambridge line supports a narrow, true point about broadcast hygiene. Nothing more. And SAG guidance is set locally by each council — there is no national spec, so "SAG-compliant" is not a claim you can make generically.

> **NEXT ACTION (today):** Remove this from all material. Replace with the *volunteer accountability* frame in §2.7. If an event safety officer spots the PA/radio conflation — and it takes ten seconds — the credibility of everything else in the deck goes with it.

### 8. Security contractors are a real channel and a SECOND sale. The reason is not margin — it is that they cannot lawfully deploy fewer stewards even if they want to.

The previously-reported argument was that contractors resist efficiency tools because they bill labour on ~5% net margins. **That reasoning does not survive.** The margin figure mislabelled EBITDA as net (£2M/£40M = 5% *EBITDA*; net profit after tax was £1.5M = **3.75% net**) `[HARD DATA — corrected]`, rested on one company via an unaudited aggregator, and the "hostile to efficiency tools" conclusion had **no source at all**. One cited source says the opposite, attributing gross-margin gains to *"better utilization and scheduling."*

**The real barrier is authority, not economics.** At UK events, steward headcount is not the contractor's to set. It is fixed in the event safety plan agreed with the licensing authority's Safety Advisory Group under the Licensing Act 2003, using Purple Guide methodology. **A contractor who wanted to deploy fewer stewards could not do so unilaterally.** So "do more with fewer people" is the wrong pitch — not because it threatens their P&L, but because it asks them for something they do not control.

Sell instead as **margin recovery on a fixed headcount** (fewer no-shows, faster shift changeover, provable coverage for the SAG) plus **liability evidence** and **ACS score**.

Also confirmed and useful: **technology does not score in stewarding tenders.** The live Durham County Council crowd management contract (Find a Tender 061699-2025, £160,000 net) is **70% price / 30% quality**, and the entire quality score is **two written questions** — Recruitment/Training/Absence, and Service Delivery. No technology question, no innovation question, no muster question `[HARD DATA — verified verbatim via the OCDS API]`. Technology appears only as a **pass/fail selection gate**: *"Systems for the Management of Lone Working, Violence & Aggression."* It is hygiene, not differentiation. Caveat: n=1, light-touch regime.

That is true, it is specific to this sector, it needs no mesh claim, and it does not touch a refuted document.

**Then the forcing function, framed correctly.** The Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025 received Royal Assent 3 April 2025; commencement is signalled for **Spring 2027** (likely April), regulated by the SIA; s.27 statutory guidance was published 15 April 2026 `[HARD DATA]`. ## 5.4 The structural killer nobody has tested

The consumer mesh may not be able to bootstrap itself `[ESTIMATE — theoretical]`.

A random geometric graph forms a giant connected component at mean degree ~4.512 (Dall & Christensen, *Phys. Rev. E* **66**, 041104, 2002). At 40,000 people over 30 hectares (0.133 people/m²), required install rates are:

| Hop range | Install rate needed to percolate site-wide |
|---|---|
| 20 m | 2.7% |
| 15 m | **4.8%** |
| 10 m | **10.8%** |
| 5 m | 43.1% |

Bodies attenuate 2.4 GHz badly, so **10–15 m is the honest through-crowd figure** — meaning roughly **5–11% of every attendee must be installed** before the network connects across the site. Below that it fragments into disconnected islands and "name who is missing" **silently fails**.

**The B2B product does not have this problem.** Guard gets a ~100% install rate inside the staff cohort because the employer mandates it, and stewards are deliberately distributed across the ground.

**This is theoretical and applies assumed geometry.** Measuring it is now the highest-value experiment available to you, because it determines whether the consumer app can function at all. See §12, week 4.

## 6.4 The zoo regulation — a real conversation opener, not a moat

**Standard 8.38** of the Standards of Modern Zoo Practice (Defra / Welsh Ministers / Scottish Ministers, under s.8 Zoo Licensing Act 1981; **in force 24 May 2027**), verbatim `[HARD DATA, p.57]`:

> *"Staff working in emergency vehicles, gate control, observation towers and elsewhere within the drive-through enclosure must maintain communication by electronic means. Such systems must have battery back-up available in case of a power cut that impacts the main communication relay. Where primary communication fails, there must be a secondary method of communication available to be used in an emergency, such as a mobile phone."*

**This was previously described as "the only requirement where the legislation literally specifies Loc8's mechanism." That is refuted.** Six problems:

1. *"Communication by electronic means"* is the generic phrase satisfied by **two-way radio** — the incumbent technology safari parks have used for forty years. No topology, no protocol, no mesh.
2. **The only technology the Standard names is a mobile phone.** Defra wrote the cheapest possible compliance answer into the sentence.
3. The "no relay to lose power" argument is a logical error. The battery clause is **conditional** — *"in case of a power cut that impacts the main communication relay."* Having no relay is **non-applicability of the clause**, not compliance with it.
4. It is **not** the only comms requirement — A9.24 (p.133, great apes) requires contact *"(via radio or other means)"* during gate operation.
5. **"Statutory" is overstated.** These are ministerially-specified standards enforced via licence conditions; the document says *"not every Standard will apply equally to all zoos."*
6. **Scope:** it binds only **drive-through enclosures** — a single-digit number of GB sites — and governs **staff voice communication**. Loc8 does not do voice.

**Standard 8.21/8.22 is the better hook and was not challenged** `[HARD DATA]`: a documented contingency procedure must cover *"the actions to be taken regarding the visiting public"* and *"processes for producing an incident report"*, reviewed within **seven days** after each drill or escape; **animal escape drills at least 4 times per year**, all recorded and reviewed. Where a zoo hosts evening events or overnight accommodation, one drill must be in twilight or dark.

**Loc8 Command's tamper-evident audit log IS the drill record. Sell the drill, not the app.**

# 9. INTERNATIONAL — ranked by ease of entry

**Do not expand internationally this year.** Every one of these is worse than the UK beachhead you have not yet won a single customer in. The ranking is what to do *when* you expand, not *whether*.

**One structural finding that changes the shape of this entirely:** because Round 1 settled on **selling connected first**, the whole spectrum/certification barrier **evaporates**. ACMA/RCM (Australia), ISED/RSS (Canada) and the AS/NZS regime all regulate the **supply of devices**. If you ship no device, none of them apply. Your phones run on hardware Apple and Samsung already certified everywhere. **The connected software product is exportable to Ireland, Australia, New Zealand and Canada with zero radio approval work** `[DERIVED]`.

**The corollary is a hardware constraint you should write into the roadmap today:** the Gateway and Anchors are the **only** things that trigger certification — and **LoRa is the worst of them**, because band plans differ (EU 868 MHz vs AU/NZ 915–928 MHz vs CA 902–928 MHz). A UK LoRa Anchor abroad is not merely uncertified, **it is on the wrong frequency** and needs a hardware variant per region.

## 10.2 The car-telematics analogy fails — but not for the reason previously given

The stated argument was that becoming an insurance rating factor requires an industry-owned validation body (Thatcham) that a solo founder cannot replicate. **The argument is self-refuting**: its own note concedes telematics reached the rating table **without** Thatcham, via insurers' proprietary driving-behaviour datasets. *Also, both headline numbers were wrong: it is **92% of UK insurers** are Thatcham Members (not 95%) and **8m+ quotes per week** use Thatcham data (not 2m+) `[HARD DATA — verified in page source and against a 2025 Wayback snapshot]`. **The correct version, and the conclusion still holds:** *both* known routes require assets you do not have.

2. **Proprietary behavioural data.** Telematics rating grew from millions of policyholder-miles. Loc8 will never generate a comparable dataset.

**Open question worth ten minutes, not a research round:** whether an existing UK certification or assurance body could carry an event-safety technology to insurer recognition by a cheaper path. Nobody has tested it.

**HSE is explicit**: Safety Advisory Groups *"do not have legal powers or responsibilities, and are not empowered to approve or prohibit events from taking place"* `[HARD DATA]`.
